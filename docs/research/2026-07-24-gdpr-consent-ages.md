# GDPR Article 8 ages of digital consent and what they mean for a 13+ Selftend

> Research for [wayfinder ticket #217](https://github.com/Selftend/selftend/issues/217),
> resolved 2026-07-24. Context: Selftend is 18+ today and wants to move to 13+ where lawful,
> without ever building parental-consent infrastructure — where law would require parental
> consent at 13, the minimum age rises instead.

## 1. The legal frame (checked 2026-07-24)

GDPR Article 8(1) applies "[w]here point (a) of Article 6(1) applies, in relation to the offer of information society services directly to a child": consent-based processing is lawful where the child is at least 16, and below 16 "only if and to the extent that consent is given or authorised by the holder of parental responsibility". Member states may lower the age by law, to no lower than 13 ([Art. 8 GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-8-gdpr/)). Art. 8(2) requires "reasonable efforts" to verify parental authorisation; Art. 8(3) says paragraph 1 "shall not affect the general contract law of Member States such as the rules on validity, formation or effect of a contract in relation to a child". Recital 38 adds that "[t]he consent of the holder of parental responsibility should not be necessary in the context of preventive or counselling services offered directly to a child" ([Recital 38](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-38/)).

EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 on consent, §7.1 (paras 124–151), confirm Art. 8 applies **only** when two conditions are both met (para 127): the processing relates to an information society service offered directly to a child, **and** the processing is based on consent. A service that credibly restricts itself to 18+ is not "offered directly to a child" (para 130) — that is Selftend's position today; moving to 13+ ends it. Para 150 repeats Recital 38 as an operative reading: "consent by a parent or guardian is not required in the context of preventive or counselling services offered directly to a child" (example given: online child-protection chat). Para 151 keeps national contract law entirely outside the guidelines' scope ([Guidelines 05/2020 PDF](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_202005_consent_en.pdf), checked 2026-07-24).

## 3. Which of Selftend's lawful bases does Article 8 bite?

Selftend's table (docs/policies.md): core processing on contract Art. 6(1)(b); native and web-push reminders on consent Art. 6(1)(a); sensitive self-help content on explicit consent Art. 9(2)(a); auth event logs on legitimate interest Art. 6(1)(f).

**(c) Reminders — consent, Art. 6(1)(a): yes, Art. 8 applies squarely.** This is the textbook case: consent basis + information society service offered directly to a child. Below the national age, reminder consent is invalid without parental authorisation (EDPB 05/2020 paras 125–127, 133). Since Selftend will not build parental consent, reminders cannot be offered to users below the national consent age.

**(a) Sensitive self-help content — explicit consent, Art. 9(2)(a): treat as within Art. 8's reach (prudent reading; formally contested).** Art. 8's text names only Art. 6(1)(a), and EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 are silent on the Art. 8 ↔ Art. 9(2)(a) interaction (Art. 9 appears only in passing, fn. 2 and fn. 10) — no EDPB statement either extends or excludes it (checked 2026-07-24). Three arguments make the prudent reading near-inescapable in practice:

1. Special-category processing needs **both** an Art. 6 basis and an Art. 9(2) condition (CJEU C-252/21 _Meta Platforms v Bundeskartellamt_, 2023, on the Art. 6/Art. 9 relationship). Contract is not an Art. 9(2) condition, so for a wellness app the realistic condition is 9(2)(a) explicit consent — meaning consent is genuinely load-bearing even where the Art. 6 basis is contract.
2. Explicit consent must still be _valid_ consent under Art. 4(11)/Art. 7. Art. 8 is precisely the legislature's judgment that a child below the national age cannot give valid consent for ISS processing on their own; a fortiori for the _higher_ bar of explicit consent to health data. A below-age child's explicit consent is at serious risk of being invalid, collapsing the Art. 9 condition and rendering the processing unlawful (cf. EDPB 05/2020 para 133 logic).
3. The ICO states it directly for the UK: the age-13 rule applies where you rely on consent "including for any non-core processing, cookies or similar technologies or **processing of special category data**" (ICO Children's Code, [Annex C: Lawful basis](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/annex-c-lawful-basis-for-processing/), checked 2026-07-24).

Thought records in a CBT app are health data: mood logs, anxiety content and similar mental-health inputs are special-category by context (ICO special-category guidance treats mental-health information as health data; same position in EDPB material on health data — checked 2026-07-24). So the core product itself rides on Art. 9(2)(a), and **the national consent age is the effective floor for the whole app**, not just for reminders.

**(b) Core processing — contract, Art. 6(1)(b): Art. 8 does not apply, but national contract-law capacity rules do.** Art. 8(3) and EDPB para 151 keep minors' contractual capacity a purely national question. Minors below majority generally have limited capacity; many systems validate everyday, age-appropriate, zero-cost transactions (e.g. Germany BGB §§106–110), but whether a data-funded or account-based service contract with a 13-year-old is valid varies and is nowhere harmonised. CNIL Recommendation 1 (2021) proposes minors 15+ be able to conclude contracts for online services adapted to young users — implying scepticism below 15 in France. The ICO likewise warns that relying on contract with children requires the child to have the competence to understand and enter the contract (Annex C). Practical consequence: contract escapes Art. 8 but does **not** deliver a clean sub-consent-age path, and it cannot substitute for the Art. 9 condition anyway (see (a)).

**Auth logs — legitimate interest, Art. 6(1)(f): Art. 8 does not apply.** But the balancing test must give extra weight to children's interests (Art. 6(1)(f) "in particular where the data subject is a child"; Recital 38). Security/abuse-prevention logging comfortably passes; no change needed.

**The Recital 38 "preventive or counselling services" carve-out — real but unreliable, except in the UK.** EDPB 05/2020 para 150 treats it as operative: parental consent "is not required in the context of preventive or counselling services offered directly to a child". A guided CBT self-help app has a colourable claim to "preventive" status. However: in the EU it rests on a recital, its scope is untested (EDPB's sole example is child-protection chat), and no DPA has blessed a consumer wellness app under it. In the **UK it is statutory**: UK GDPR Art. 8's definition of ISS "does not include preventive or counselling services" (carried over from DPA 2018 s. 9(2); reflected in [ICO guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/children-and-the-uk-gdpr-old/what-are-the-rules-about-an-iss-and-consent/), checked 2026-07-24). Treat as a fallback argument, not a plan.

## 4. Answer: the lowest age floor without parental consent

- **Uniform floor across all 31 markets: 16.** Driven by the ten age-16 countries (Germany, Croatia, Hungary, Ireland, Luxembourg, Netherlands, Poland, Romania, Slovakia, Liechtenstein). Simple, but keeps 13–15-year-olds out everywhere, including the eleven age-13 markets.
- **Per-country floor: the national age of digital consent in each market** — 13 in Belgium, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Latvia, Malta, Portugal, Sweden, Norway, Iceland and the UK; 14 in Austria, Bulgaria, Cyprus, Italy, Lithuania, Spain; 15 in Czechia, France, Greece, Slovenia; 16 in the ten above. At or above that age the user consents alone: reminders-consent is valid (Art. 8 satisfied by age), explicit consent for thought records is valid, contract capacity concerns are at their weakest, and no parental flow is ever needed.
- The floor is set by the **Art. 9(2)(a)/6(1)(a) consent bases**, not by contract: dropping reminders below the consent age would not lower the floor, because the core thought-record content itself needs explicit consent.

### Design implications for Selftend

1. Gate signup by **self-declared age band per country of residence** against the table above; store only "meets threshold for country X", not date of birth — consistent with EDPB 05/2020 paras 132–135 (proportionate, low-data age checks; self-declaration acceptable in low-risk cases) and data minimization. Given health data + teen users, pure self-declaration is the _minimum_; be ready to justify it in a DPIA.
2. A **DPIA is required before the 13+ launch**: sensitive data + vulnerable data subjects hits two WP248/EDPB DPIA criteria.
3. Once 13+ anywhere, the service is "offered directly to a child" — the UK **Children's Code applies in full** (15 standards: best interests, high-privacy defaults, DPIA, no nudging, data minimization — applies to services _likely to be accessed_ by under-18s regardless of consent age, checked 2026-07-24). France: CNIL's [8 recommendations](https://www.cnil.fr/en/cnil-publishes-8-recommendations-enhance-protection-children-online) (2021). Ireland: DPC ["Fundamentals"](https://www.dataprotection.ie/sites/default/files/uploads/2021-12/Fundamentals%20for%20a%20Child-Oriented%20Approach%20to%20Data%20Processing_FINAL_EN.pdf) (Dec 2021, "floor of protection" for all users unless age is verified risk-based). Selftend's existing guardrails (no ads, no tracking, optional non-punitive reminders, no social feed) already align unusually well.
4. Keep the crisis/safety resources age-appropriate and visible — DPC Fundamentals and the Children's Code both expect child-specific transparency (plain-language privacy info for teens).

### Open questions / assumptions (marked per docs rules)

- **Assumption:** thought records are Art. 9 health data. Defensible to argue some entries are not, but content is user-controlled and mental-health-framed — plan on Art. 9 applying.
- **Contested reading:** Art. 8's formal application to Art. 9(2)(a) explicit consent has no CJEU or EDPB ruling either way; this doc adopts the prudent view (invalid-consent logic + ICO position). If a future EDPB children's-data guideline (in the EDPB work programme; not adopted as of 2026-07-24) resolves it the other way, the per-country floor for the _core_ app could theoretically drop to contract-capacity limits — do not build on that.
- **Open question:** whether Selftend qualifies as a Recital 38 "preventive or counselling service" in the EU. Strong in the UK (statutory); untested elsewhere. Revisit if a DPA opines.
- **Open question:** contract capacity of 13–15-year-olds for a free ToS varies by member state and was not researched per-country; irrelevant while the floor is set by consent ages, but would matter if the contested reading above ever resolves against Art. 8 reaching Art. 9.
- **(C)-marked table rows** rest on consolidated mappings (euConsent, Library of Congress) rather than the national gazette text; before launching in any (C) country at its stated age, spot-check its statute. All (P) rows were followed to primary or national-DPA-adjacent sources on 2026-07-24.
