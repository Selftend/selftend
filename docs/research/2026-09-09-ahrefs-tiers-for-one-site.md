# What each Ahrefs tier buys for one verified site, and whether Site Audit renders JavaScript

Research for [What each Ahrefs tier buys for one verified site, and whether Site Audit renders JavaScript](https://github.com/Selftend/selftend/issues/2283), under map [selftend.org readable by search engines](https://github.com/Selftend/selftend/issues/2281).

**All prices and product facts checked 2026-09-09**, against Ahrefs' own pricing, legal, product and help-centre pages only. No blog round-ups, no reseller pages. Ahrefs moves its plan limits often (the help centre's plan-comparison article was "Updated this week" on the day of checking), so verify again before paying.

This document **does not choose a tier**. It feeds the owner ruling the map records as "Ahrefs on a paid plan — the tier is chosen on the research ticket's facts."

## Headline

1. **The free tier already does the whole indexability job for one verified site.** Site Audit crawls the verified project with 5,000 crawl credits a month, has an **"Execute JavaScript"** crawl setting (off by default; nothing on any Ahrefs page gates it to a paid plan), and shows raw vs rendered HTML per URL. Eight public routes on a single-page shell use eight credits.
2. **"Ahrefs Webmaster Tools" has been renamed "Ahrefs Free."** Same product; the old URL still resolves and carries the banner.
3. **Ahrefs Web Analytics is an analytics tracking service by any plain reading**: a `<script>` from `https://analytics.ahrefs.com/analytics.js` in the page `<head>`, sending page URL, referrer, user agent, IP-derived city, language and a daily-salted hash of IP+UA to Ahrefs, which also uses the aggregate for its own "data studies" and to train its traffic-estimation models. No cookies — but the privacy-policy line is "analytics tracking services", not "cookies". It stays out, as the map already rules.
4. **What paid answers that free cannot is one thing: the demand side.** Keywords Explorer (search volume for the queries the eight routes could answer) and Site Explorer on domains you do not own (do comparable free CBT apps receive organic search traffic at all). Both arrive at **Starter, $29/month, monthly-only**. Lite ($129) and above add history, seats, rank-tracking volume and API — none of which the map's two decisions wait on.

### Verification methods

Four, per the help centre, verbatim:

1. **Google Search Console (Ahrefs' "recommended")** — "Connect your Google Account and allow access to your search console data to verify your website." Any property verified in GSC is "automatically verified in Ahrefs." Note this is an OAuth grant of the owner's GSC data to Ahrefs — an owner-account decision, not a user-data one. The project URL must match GSC exactly (www vs non-www), which matters here because both hosts serve the app with no redirect.
2. **DNS record** — "Add a verification .txt record to your domain's DNS settings." Can take "up to 48 hours" to propagate. No change to the deployed site. The cleanest fit for a Cloudflare-hosted zone.
3. **HTML file** — "Download the HTML verification file and upload it to your domain's root folder." Would be a static asset under `public/`; the record "must remain on your site."
4. **HTML tag** — "Add the meta tag provided to the `<head>` section of your website's homepage." One inert `<meta>` line in `public/index.html`; not a script, no CSP change.

Sources: [Verifying ownership of your project or website](https://help.ahrefs.com/en/articles/3275938-verifying-ownership-of-your-project-or-website), [How to add a project in your Dashboard](https://help.ahrefs.com/en/articles/1433362-how-to-add-a-project-in-your-dashboard). Checked 2026-09-09.

## Finding 2 — Site Audit renders JavaScript, as a setting, off by default, on every tier

This is the fact the map most needed, and it is settled.

- **It is a toggle.** The Site Audit settings guide lists the Crawl settings section and describes the example configuration as "The crawler will not render javascript when checking any pages. But, it will check image, CSS, and javascript links for any issues." Its FAQ then says: "If you wish to just crawl your website fully, you can leave the settings by default as they are. We would recommend that the **'Execute Javascript' toggle be turned on** in the case your website heavily uses javascript to generate the content in your pages."
- **Default is off.** No page states the default in so many words, but three of Ahrefs' own pages say it the long way: the settings guide's default example does not render JS and tells JS-heavy sites to turn the toggle on; the Patches article says the option "is automatically enabled in the projects where you set up deployment" and warns "your crawls will take longer if you had this option turned off before"; the JS-crawling product post says the setting "must be turned on in the crawl settings." Treat it as off until switched, and switch it before the first crawl of `selftend.org`.
- **Not gated by plan.** The settings guide names exactly two plan- or status-gated crawl settings: robots.txt override / mobile user agent ("only available for verified projects") and HTTP authentication ("available for Advanced subscribers and higher"). Execute JavaScript carries no such note, and the free tier's own copy says "The tools and data are the same" as paid, with paid differing in "higher data allowances." The crawl-credits article says only 200-OK HTML pages spend credits, with no rendering surcharge mentioned anywhere.
- **What rendering gives you.** Per URL, Site Audit shows "Raw HTML, Rendered HTML, or the Page text seen by our crawler," and the compare dropdown can diff "between raw and rendered versions" — precisely the empty-`#root` vs rendered-app question for a JS-only SPA. The product post says the crawler waits about 3 seconds before snapshotting, and that JS crawls are slower; the bot page shows the mobile user agent as a Chrome 151 string (`Mozilla/5.0 (Linux; Android 13) AppleWebKit/537.36 (KHTML, like Gecko) Chrome/151.0.7922.173 Mobile Safari/537.36 (compatible; AhrefsSiteAudit/6.1; +http://ahrefs.com/robot/site-audit)`).
- **Seeding without a sitemap.** URL sources default to "Website" (the project URL) and "Auto-detected sitemaps" from `robots.txt` or `/sitemap.xml`. `selftend.org` has neither file today, so discovery of the other seven routes depends on links the rendered landing page exposes — or on the **"Custom URL list"** source, where the eight routes can be pasted directly.

Sources: [How should I configure my Site Audit settings?](https://help.ahrefs.com/en/articles/9082329-how-should-i-configure-my-site-audit-settings), [How Patches work in Site Audit](https://help.ahrefs.com/en/articles/9775727-how-patches-work-in-site-audit), [Site Audit now crawls JavaScript (Ahrefs product post)](https://ahrefs.com/blog/site-audit-crawls-javascript), [How to compare changes between two crawls](https://help.ahrefs.com/en/articles/5192078-how-to-compare-changes-between-two-crawls-in-site-audit), [AhrefsSiteAudit bot page](https://ahrefs.com/robot/site-audit), [How are Site Audit credits spent?](https://help.ahrefs.com/en/articles/3119402-how-are-site-audit-credits-spent). ## Finding 4 — Ahrefs Web Analytics is an analytics tracking service

The map already rules it out; this finding says why the ruling is correct on the facts, not merely on caution.

**Mechanism.** It is a JavaScript snippet loaded from **`https://analytics.ahrefs.com/analytics.js`**, which "needs to be installed in the `<head>` element of your website." Ahrefs' own troubleshooting page tells sites with a Content-Security-Policy to "add `https://analytics.ahrefs.com` to the CSP `script-src` directive" — which, for `public/_headers` (`script-src 'self'` plus one hash), would be the first third-party script host the site has ever allowed. A Google Tag Manager route also exists. Script size is "2 KB".

**What it collects and stores**, from Ahrefs' own list: page URL of every view; referrer; user agent ("to derive information about the operating systems, devices, and browsers" and to count unique visitors); location to city level derived from the IP address; the `Accept-Language` header and browser language preferences; and, automatically, "page views, link clicks and form submissions" — outbound-link clicks and form submissions are "tracked automatically without requiring setup." Unique visitors are counted with `hash(daily_salt + ip_address + user_agent)`, salt rotated every 24 hours, raw IP "never stored in our database or logs." Visits are sessions closed by 30 minutes of inactivity. Custom events can be added by CSS class or `window.AhrefsAnalytics.sendEvent()`.

**Cookies.** "We do not use cookies, collect any personal data, or store any personally identifiable information by default (we're working on a solution with cookies for advanced conversion tracking)." So: no cookies today; a cookie-based mode is announced.

**Who else uses the data.** "From time to time, we may publish studies and reports based on anonymized and aggregated data," and "The aggregated data we collect will also be used to improve our services, such as enhancing traffic prediction and traffic estimation models." And: "you remain responsible for complying with your own obligations under the applicable Privacy regulations and the European Union General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)."

**Verdict.** The privacy policy (`src/i18n/locales/en/policies.json`, pinned to `policyVersion` by `src/features/policies/policy-content.test.ts`) says: "We do not use advertising SDKs, **analytics tracking services**, behavioral profiling tools, or social media pixels." A third-party-hosted script that reports each visitor's page views, clicks, form submissions, device, language and city to a vendor that also reuses the aggregate for its own products **is an analytics tracking service**. Cookielessness does not change that; the policy line is not about cookies, and `docs/analytics.md` treats cookieless Plausible/Umami as analytics too. Adding it would mean a privacy-policy amendment, a `policyVersion` bump and forced re-consent of every user, and a CSP change — exactly the cost the map's "Out of scope" section names. It stays out.

Sources: [About Ahrefs Web Analytics](https://help.ahrefs.com/en/articles/10247870-about-ahrefs-web-analytics), [Tracked events in Ahrefs Web Analytics](https://help.ahrefs.com/en/articles/11381932-tracked-events-in-ahrefs-web-analytics), [Troubleshooting "snippet isn't found on your website"](https://help.ahrefs.com/en/articles/10631520-troubleshooting-ahrefs-web-analytics-snippet-isn-t-found-on-your-website), [Web Analytics product page](https://ahrefs.com/web-analytics). Checked 2026-09-09. Ahrefs' [Privacy Policy](https://ahrefs.com/legal/privacy-policy) was also read; it covers Ahrefs' own services and does not describe the analytics snippet's data separately.

## Finding 5 — the honest comparison

The map decides two things: an **indexability spec for eight existing public routes**, and a **ruling on whether organic search becomes a worked channel**. Against those:

**Free answers the indexability spec completely.** Site Audit with Execute JavaScript on, seeded with the eight routes, reports what a rendering crawler sees: titles, descriptions, canonicals, `noindex`, `lang`/`hreflang`, structured data, status codes on unknown paths, raw-vs-rendered diff. Google Search Console — already in scope — reports what Google actually indexed and which queries surfaced the site. Nothing a paid tier adds (history, seats, export rows, API units, more crawl credits, rank-tracking volume) bears on eight pages that currently rank for nothing. Lite's six months of history is history of a site with none.

**Paid answers one thing free cannot: demand.** The free tier can only look inward, at a domain you own. The channel ruling may want two outward-facing facts:

1. **Keywords Explorer** — do the queries the eight routes could honestly answer ("CBT app", "thought record app", "free cognitive behavioural therapy programme", the Bulgarian equivalents) have any search volume, and how hard are they. Free has no Keywords Explorer at all.
2. **Site Explorer on unverified domains** — do comparable free self-help apps receive organic search traffic, and from which pages. Free is verified-domains-only.

Both arrive at **Starter: $29, monthly, cancel any time, 200 credits a month across Site Explorer and Keywords Explorer**, no annual option, and a stated "Features and limits on this plan may be changed without prior notice." Nothing above Starter adds anything either decision waits on; Lite's extras are seats, history, 750 tracked keywords and API access.

Name the thing, then: **the paid tier buys the demand-side evidence for the channel ruling, and only that.** Whether the ruling needs evidence at all is the owner's call — `docs/positioning.md` refuses growth as an ambition and `docs/marketing-plan.md` already serialises a queue, so the ruling may be made on principle, in which case the answer to "what does paid add" is **nothing**, and Ahrefs Free plus Search Console is the whole instrument set.

Two further notes for the ruling:

- Search Console's Performance report already gives real impressions, clicks and average position for queries the site _does_ appear for, free, so Rank Tracker (any tier) duplicates GSC for a site with a handful of pages.
- Whichever tier is chosen, the site changes are identical: one DNS TXT record (or one meta tag) to verify, and the Execute JavaScript toggle on before the first crawl. Nothing about the tier alters the indexability spec.

## What was not verified

- The exact annual prices as rendered by the pricing page's billing toggle — the static HTML only carried the monthly figures and the "save up to 17%" line; the annual numbers above are computed from Ahrefs' own "10 months for 12" rule.
- Whether Execute JavaScript exists on the free tier as a _visible_ toggle was inferred from the absence of any plan note on Ahrefs' pages, not observed in a free account. Ahrefs' free-tier copy says the tools are "the same." If it turns out gated, Starter is the floor and the cost is $29 for one month.
- Ahrefs' 48-hour refund window is quoted by third parties only; no Ahrefs page states it.
