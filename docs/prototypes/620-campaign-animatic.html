Direction — what this variant argues

Motion: one idea on screen at a time; everything arrives by slow crossfade and a barely-visible scale, timed like an exhale. No cuts.

Type: brand face at conversational size; the brand line is the only big typographic moment.

Sound-bed: warm solo piano over room tone; one swell under the brand line; VO sparse — two lines at the open, one at the close.

Silence test: screen labels and captions carry beats 3–4 with no VO at all.

Motion: typography is the footage. Hard-ish cuts land on the beat; device cards are supporting evidence that slides in beside the words.

Type: oversized weight-800 lines with a violet emphasis word per beat; dark ground for maximum type contrast.

Sound-bed: soft synth pulse ≈72 bpm; each cut lands on a pulse; VO reads exactly the on-screen lines.

Silence test: strongest of the three — the message *is* the text.

Motion: one continuous take. A single device stays on screen while the app moves inside it (tap ripples, screen changes); at the brand beat the phone steps aside for tablet + web.

Type: almost none on the ground — subtitles carry the words; the product is the typography.

Sound-bed: ambient pad plus soft UI ticks synced to the taps; conversational VO carries the meaning.

Silence test: weakest without captions — this language leans on narration, so captions are non-negotiable.

What to react to (a mixed answer is expected)

  1. Language — breath-led (A), type-led (B), or device-led (C)? Or e.g. "C's continuous take with B's brand-line typography"?
  2. Narration tone — sparse (A), structured echo of the text (B), or conversational (C)?
  3. Ground — light app-surface, dark, or per-platform cut?
  4. Pace — do B's cuts feel un-calm? Does A drag at 13s?
  5. Reduced-motion mode — flip the toggle and replay: does the restrained version still communicate? (This becomes the actual reduced-motion deliverable rule.)
  6. Sound-bed direction — which described bed fits the brand? (No audio in this artifact by design.)

Beat structure shared by all variants: Hook → Check-in → Breathe → Journal → Brand → CTA, ≈13s total, composed center-safe for 4:5 cropping (toggle the guides). Copy honours the map: age-neutral, no outcome or medical claims, canonical brand line, CTA "Try Selftend on Android, iOS, or the web". Screens shown: check-in, box breathing, journal — with the "no streaks, no pressure" and "private to you" trust cues surfaced in-product rather than claimed in VO.
